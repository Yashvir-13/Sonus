# Design System: Living Manuscript

## Theme & Aesthetic
- **Concept:** An interactive, experimental musical instrument masquerading as sheet music.
- **Colors (Parchment & Ink):** 
  - Background: Off-white/sepia (`#F4F1EA`) mimicking high-quality parchment paper.
  - Structural Lines (Staves/Borders): Deep charcoal (`#2C2A29`).
  - Accents/Active States/Errors: Rich crimson ink (`#9A2A2A`).
  - Text: Deep charcoal (`#2C2A29`) for high contrast readability.

## Typography
- **Editorial Mix:**
  - Headers & Qualitative Annotations: Elegant Serif (e.g., Playfair Display or Instrument Serif).
  - Technical Data (BPM, Cents, Timestamps): Crisp Monospace (e.g., Geist Mono, JetBrains Mono).
  - Body Text: Clean, readable serif or a complementary sans-serif for UI labels if needed.

## Layout & Navigation
- **Immersive Canvas:** No traditional navbars or sidebars. The app should feel like a fluid digital music stand.
- Elements align to an invisible "staff" grid—using 5 horizontal structural lines for major data alignments.

## Components & Visuals
- **Icons:** Use musical glyphs (SMuFL standard where possible) like fermatas, codas, and staccato dots instead of standard UI icons (hamburgers, cogs).
- **Live Practice Ribbon:** Visual feedback is a continuous flowing ribbon tracing pitch against the target staff.
- **Particles:** Active states and feedback emit sophisticated particles (e.g., realistic ink splatters or charcoal dust).
- **Post-Session Review:** The canvas zooms out into an architectural blueprint timeline. Errors are overlaid as elegant, crimson "editor's marks" (circled flat notes, slashed rushed measures).

## Guidelines
- Avoid generic SaaS UI patterns (standard cards, drop shadows, pill buttons).
- Use stark lines, high contrast, and structural layouts.
- Keep the interface minimal to avoid distracting the musician while playing.
